# Safe Home Care Basics: What You Can Do and What NOT to Self-Medicate

**Specialty: General Medicine**

Most minor illnesses and injuries can be safely managed at home with rest, fluids, and simple first aid. The skill is knowing what is safe to handle and when to stop treating yourself and see a doctor. This guide covers the basics — and the things you should never self-medicate.

## Safe home care that helps

- **Rest:** Give your body time to recover. Sleep is one of the most effective forms of self-care.
- **Hydration:** Drink water regularly, especially during a fever or vomiting; aim for light-coloured urine as a simple guide to good hydration.
- **Paracetamol:** Use for fever or mild pain only as per the doctor or the label, and never exceed the recommended dose or frequency.
- **Cold compress:** For minor sprains and bumps, apply a cold compress for short periods to reduce swelling, and keep the area elevated.
- Warm fluids and gentle light meals help most viral illnesses settle on their own.

## What NOT to self-medicate

These need a doctor's assessment first:
- **Antibiotics** — use only when a doctor prescribes them; antibiotics do not work for viral infections, and misuse is harmful.
- **New or different painkillers** for unexplained pain — do not mask symptoms of an unknown cause.
- **Steroids or strong anti-inflammatories** without medical advice.
- **Medicine for someone else's symptoms** on the basis of an online description — what helped one person may be wrong or unsafe for another.
- **Any medicine for children, pregnant women, or the elderly** without proper guidance — dosing and safety differ.
- **Leftover pills** from an old illness or another person's prescription.

Always check the label, and if you have liver or kidney disease, are pregnant, or take other medicines, ask a doctor or pharmacist before using anything.

## When to step up — see a doctor

- Fever that is very high, or an illness that is not improving after the usual few days.
- New symptoms that worry you, unexpectedly severe pain, or pain that will not settle.
- Symptoms in a child, older adult, pregnant woman, or someone with a long-term illness.

## When to seek urgent care — RED FLAGS

**Stop home care and get urgent help if you notice:**
- Sudden, severe pain, difficulty breathing, confusion, or difficulty waking.
- A very high fever, a stiff neck with a severe headache, or a rash that does not fade when pressed.
- Severe bleeding, seizures, or any sign of stroke or heart attack (see the Emergency Red Flags guide).

Home care is for mild, familiar problems. When in doubt, or when symptoms are severe or persistent, get professional advice rather than treating yourself further.

# Emergency Red Flags: When to Get Help Immediately

**Specialty: General Medicine**

Some symptoms are medical emergencies: they need immediate help, not waiting at home. Reading this before an emergency makes it easier to act fast. If you think someone might be having an emergency, call for help right away — time is critical.

## Stroke — remember FAST

A stroke damages the brain quickly, and every minute counts.

- **F — Face:** Does one side of the face droop or feel numb? Ask the person to smile — is the smile uneven?
- **A — Arm:** Is one arm weak or numb? Ask them to raise both arms — does one drift down?
- **S — Speech:** Is speech slurred, or is the person unable to speak or hard to understand?
- **T — Time:** If any of these signs appear, **call emergency services immediately** and get them to a hospital. Note the time symptoms began.

**If any FAST sign appears, even if it goes away, this is an emergency — call help immediately.**

## Heart attack

- Crushing, squeezing, or heavy pressure in the centre of the chest.
- Pain spreading to the arm (usually left), neck, jaw, or back.
- Cold sweats, nausea, shortness of breath, or lightheadedness.

**Chest pain like this is an emergency — call for help immediately. Do not wait to see if it passes.**

## Other emergency situations — act immediately

- **Breathing difficulty:** Struggling to breathe, choking, gasping, or blue-tinged lips — call emergency services at once.
- **Severe bleeding:** Bleeding that will not stop with firm pressure, or heavy blood loss.
- **Seizures:** A seizure lasting longer than five minutes, one that repeats without recovery, or if the person stops breathing.
- **Suicidal thoughts:** Thoughts of harming yourself or someone else are a crisis — get urgent professional help now and do not leave the person alone.
- **Unconsciousness:** Someone who is unresponsive or hard to wake.
- **Severe allergic reaction:** Sudden swelling of the face, lips, or throat, with trouble breathing.

## What to do while waiting for help

- Call your local emergency number and clearly state the emergency and your location.
- Do **not** give food, drink, or medicine to an unconscious person.
- Keep the person calm, loosen tight clothing, and stay with them.
- If you are alone and think you are having a stroke or heart attack, call for help before doing anything else.

**If this list leaves you unsure, err on the side of getting help — a false alarm is far better than a dangerous delay.**

# Bones, Joints, and Back: Pain, Sprains, Fractures, and Posture

**Specialty: Orthopedics**

Back pain, joint pain, sprains, and suspected fractures are extremely common. Many settle with simple care and time, but some need an orthopedist — and a few need urgent attention. Learning the difference protects you from both unnecessary panic and dangerous delay.

## Home care that often helps

- **Back and mild joint pain:** Gentle rest for the first day or two, avoiding heavy lifting, and gradually returning to normal movement. Staying reasonably active is usually better than spending days in bed.
- **Sprains:** Rest the injured joint, apply a cold compress for short periods to reduce swelling, keep the area elevated, and give it time to heal.
- **Posture:** Frequent breaks from sitting, an ergonomic desk setup, and regular walking can reduce everyday back and neck strain.
- **Pain relief:** Use paracetamol or an anti-inflammatory only as per the doctor or the label. Some back pain improves with light stretching, but stop if it makes things worse.

## When to see an orthopedist

- Pain that is constant, intense, or worse at night or when lying down.
- Pain that spreads down the leg, especially below the knee, or joint pain that limits daily activity.
- Pain that has not improved after a week or so of home care, or swelling and deformity after an injury.
- Recurrent sprains or joints that 'give way'.

## When you may need an X-ray or scan

An X-ray is most useful after significant trauma, to check for fractures and dislocation. Your doctor decides whether imaging is needed — you do not need an X-ray for every ache. Seek ortho evaluation after a fall or impact with severe pain, swelling, or an inability to bear weight.

## When to seek urgent care — RED FLAGS

**Get urgent help immediately if back pain or an injury comes with:**
- New loss of bowel or bladder control (this is an emergency sign).
- Sudden weakness, numbness, or tingling in the legs, especially both legs or the groin area.
- Back pain after major trauma such as a car crash or bad fall.
- Back pain with a fever or unexplained weight loss.
- Difficulty standing, walking, or breathing after an injury.

Pain accompanied by a fever could signal a spinal infection, and sudden severe weakness or saddle-area numbness needs urgent ortho and emergency evaluation.

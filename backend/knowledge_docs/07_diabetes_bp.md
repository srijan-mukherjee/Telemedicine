# Diabetes and Blood Pressure: Symptoms, Warning Signs, and Basics

**Specialty: General Medicine (with Cardiology input)**

Diabetes and high blood pressure often travel together and, if uncontrolled, can silently damage the heart, kidneys, eyes, and blood vessels. Many people with high sugar or high blood pressure feel fine at first, which is why regular checks matter. This guide covers the basics plus the emergency warning signs.

## Diabetes basics

- **Too high sugar:** Frequent thirst, passing urine often, unexplained weight loss, blurred vision, and slow-healing wounds can all be signs of high blood sugar.
- **Hypoglycemia (low sugar):** This can happen in people taking diabetes medicines. Early signs include shakiness, sweating, a fast heartbeat, hunger, dizziness, and irritability. If it worsens, a person may become confused, clumsy, or slur their speech.

### Hypoglycemia RED FLAGS
If someone with diabetes becomes confused, cannot speak clearly, has seizures, or loses consciousness, this is an emergency — get help immediately. Treat very low sugar right away.

## Blood pressure basics

- High blood pressure usually has **no symptoms**, so regular measurement is essential.
- A **hypertensive crisis** means a reading of **180/120 mm Hg or higher**. On its own, a high number needs prompt medical review; with symptoms, it is an emergency.

### Hypertensive crisis RED FLAGS
A blood pressure of 180/120 or higher **with** chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, vision changes, weakness, numbness, or difficulty speaking is a hypertensive emergency — call for urgent help now.

## Care that helps

- Follow your doctor's guidance on diet, physical activity, medication, and monitoring. Never stop prescribed medicines on your own.
- Check your sugar and blood pressure as your doctor advises, and keep a written log to show on visits.
- For heart and vessel complications of diabetes and blood pressure, a cardiologist may be part of your care team. Keep follow-up appointments.

## When to see a doctor

- Newly suspected symptoms of high sugar or high blood pressure.
- Repeated readings above your target, or unexplained swings in your numbers.
- Any sign of nerve, eye, kidney, or heart trouble — numbness, vision changes, or leg swelling.

Remember: diabetes and blood pressure are managed day by day with a doctor's guidance, and catching complications early protects your heart, kidneys, eyes, and nerves.

# Mental Health: Anxiety, Depression, and Stress

**Specialty: General Medicine**

Anxiety, depression, and stress are real and treatable health conditions, not personal weaknesses. Many people experience periods of low mood, worry, or overwhelm, especially during exam seasons, job changes, or major life transitions. Reaching out for help is a sign of strength, and recovery is possible with proper support.

## What these can look like

- **Stress:** Feeling overwhelmed, irritable, tense, or unable to switch off; it often shows up as poor sleep, headaches, or a churning stomach.
- **Anxiety:** Persistent worry, restlessness, racing thoughts, and sometimes physical signs such as a racing heart, sweating, or shortness of breath.
- **Depression:** A low mood most of the day nearly every day, loss of interest in things you used to enjoy, low energy, changes in appetite or sleep, and feelings of hopelessness or worthlessness.

## Self-care that helps

- **Talk to someone you trust** — family, friends, or a counsellor. You do not need to cope alone.
- **Keep a routine** around sleep, meals, and light physical activity (even a daily walk helps).
- **Limit stress triggers** where you can, take regular breaks from screens and social media, and break big tasks into small steps.
- **Practise breathing or relaxation** during anxious moments; slow, deep breaths can help calm the body.

## When to see a doctor

- Low mood, worry, or stress that lasts more than two weeks and affects your work, study, sleep, or relationships.
- Anxiety that interferes with daily life, or physical symptoms that keep returning.
- Difficulty functioning, or symptoms that are getting worse despite self-care.

## Crisis signs — seek professional help urgently

**Get urgent professional help immediately if you or someone you know:**
- Has thoughts of self-harm or suicide.
- Says they plan to harm themselves or others.
- Has stopped eating, drinking, or caring for basic needs.
- Shows signs of harming themselves.

If there is any risk of immediate harm, do not leave the person alone and do not delay — contact emergency services or take them to a hospital. National helplines such as India's Tele-MANAS (14416) provide free 24/7 support. In an emergency, call medical services right away.

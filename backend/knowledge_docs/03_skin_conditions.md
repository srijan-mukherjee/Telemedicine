# Skin Conditions: Rashes, Acne, Eczema, Fungal Infections, and Allergic Reactions

**Specialty: Dermatology**

Skin conditions such as rashes, acne, eczema, fungal infections, and allergic reactions are very common. Many mild cases improve with gentle skin care and over-the-counter products, but persistent, spreading, or painful lesions should be checked by a dermatologist.

## Common types and what they look like

- **Acne:** Blocked pores causing blackheads, whiteheads, pimples, or deeper painful lumps. Mild acne is common in teenagers and young adults.
- **Eczema:** Dry, itchy, often scaly patches, frequently on the inner elbows, behind the knees, or on the hands. It tends to flare and settle.
- **Fungal infections:** Itchy, ring-shaped, red, scaly patches (ringworm), athlete's foot between the toes, or clusters of small itchy bumps that do not heal with normal acne treatment.
- **Allergic skin reactions:** Red, itchy areas that appear after contact with a substance, a food, or a medicine; these can range from a mild rash to hives.

## Home care that helps

- Keep the skin clean and dry; pat it dry rather than rubbing.
- Moisturise regularly if your skin is dry or eczema-prone, and avoid harsh soaps and very hot water.
- For fungal infections, keep the area dry and change socks and clothing regularly; use antifungal products only as per the doctor or the label.
- Avoid scratching, which can break skin and cause infection.

## When to see a doctor (Dermatology)

- A rash that lasts more than a few weeks or keeps spreading.
- Severe or deep acne that does not respond to over-the-counter care.
- A rash that is getting worse after treatment.
- Painful, crusted, oozing, or infected-looking skin, or rapidly spreading redness.

## When to seek urgent care — RED FLAGS

**Seek urgent help if a skin problem comes with:**
- Sudden swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat (this is a sign of a serious allergic reaction).
- Difficulty breathing or wheezing with a rash.
- A rash that looks like tiny bruises or purplish spots that do not fade when pressed.
- Blisters covering a large area, or a fever with a widespread rash.

Change in a mole's shape, colour, or size deserves a dermatologist's review, even if it is not painful.

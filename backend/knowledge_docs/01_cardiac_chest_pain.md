# Chest Pain: Understanding the Warning Signs

**Specialty: Cardiology**

Chest pain is common and not every cause is serious. It can come from the heart, from a pulled muscle in the chest wall, or from acidity (gastroesophageal reflux). The key is learning how to tell them apart and knowing when chest pain may be a medical emergency.

## What helps you tell the difference

- **Cardiac (heart) pain:** Often feels like pressure, tightness, squeezing, or heaviness in the centre of the chest. It may spread to the shoulder, arm (usually the left), back, neck, or jaw. It can be accompanied by cold sweats, shortness of breath, nausea, or an overwhelming feeling of anxiety.
- **Muscle strain:** Usually feels sharper and worsens when you move, twist, press on the spot, or take a deep breath. It is typically localised to one area and is not accompanied by sweats or difficulty breathing.
- **Acidity (reflux):** Often described as burning behind the breastbone, worse after eating or when lying down, and often relieved by antacids. It may come with a sour taste or burping.

## What you can do at home

- If the chest pain feels mild and is clearly linked to movement or to a recent meal, rest, sit upright, and monitor how you feel.
- Keep a note of when it happens, what brings it on, and what relieves it, so you can describe it clearly to a doctor.
- Always get a proper diagnosis from a doctor before assuming your pain is "just acidity" or "just a strain."

## When to seek urgent care — RED FLAGS

**Treat chest pain as an emergency and seek help immediately if it:**
- Feels like crushing, pressure, or squeezing in the centre of the chest.
- Spreads to the arm, neck, jaw, or back.
- Comes with cold sweats, nausea, lightheadedness, shortness of breath, or sudden weakness.
- Starts suddenly, is severe, or lasts more than a few minutes and does not settle with rest.

Even if you are unsure whether it is from the heart, when chest pain is new, severe, or paired with sweating or breathlessness, do not wait to see if it passes. Call for emergency medical help right away. Pain that eases and returns can also be a warning sign of an impending heart problem.

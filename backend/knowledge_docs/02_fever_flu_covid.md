# Fever, Cold, Flu, and COVID-Like Symptoms: Home Care and When to Worry

**Specialty: General Medicine**

A fever usually means your body is fighting an infection such as a cold, flu, or COVID-19. In most adults, a temperature of 38°C or higher counts as a fever. Most viral illnesses improve on their own with rest and fluids, but certain symptoms need a doctor.

## Home care that helps

- **Rest** — your body heals faster when you are not overactive.
- **Drink fluids** — water is best; drink enough so your urine stays light yellow and clear, which is a simple sign you are well-hydrated.
- **Keep cool and comfortable** — light clothing and a normal room temperature are enough; you do not need to bundle up.
- **Use paracetamol or ibuprofen only as per the doctor or the label** to ease discomfort if you feel unwell. Never exceed the recommended dose.
- Watch for improvement over the next few days. Most viral fevers settle within about three days.

## When to see a doctor

- Your fever is very high (40°C or higher).
- You are still feverish after about three days or your symptoms keep getting worse.
- Shivering or chattering teeth does not settle within about an hour.
- You have a severe headache that does not improve, trouble breathing, or you feel confused or unusually drowsy.
- You recently travelled or you are pregnant and have a fever.

## When to seek urgent care — RED FLAGS

**Seek urgent medical help if a fever comes with:**
- A severe headache with a stiff neck and sensitivity to light (these can be signs of meningitis).
- A rash that does not fade when pressed.
- Trouble breathing, chest pain, difficulty waking, or frequent vomiting.
- Seizures, or confusion where the person is hard to rouse.

For COVID-like symptoms, test if advised and tell your doctor about any close contact with a confirmed case. If you belong to a high-risk group (older age, pregnancy, or chronic disease), contact your doctor early rather than waiting it out.

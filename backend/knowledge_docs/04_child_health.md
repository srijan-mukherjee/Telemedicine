# Child Health: Fever, Hydration, Vaccination, and Urgent Signs

**Specialty: Pediatrics**

Children get fevers and minor illnesses often, and most recover with calm home care. The most important jobs for a parent are keeping a sick child well-hydrated, knowing the warning signs of dehydration, and following the vaccination schedule. Always involve your paediatrician for guidance on a specific child.

## Home care that helps

- **Keep them hydrated:** Offer small, frequent sips of fluid. In younger children and infants, continue feeds. The goal is to keep urine light in colour and roughly normal in amount.
- **Let them rest** and keep clothing light if feverish. Use paracetamol at an infant-appropriate dose strictly as per the doctor or the label for higher fevers, and never exceed the recommended dose.
- **Monitor closely** during the first 24–48 hours of an illness, especially in babies.

## Dehydration — what to watch for

Early warning signs in a child include a dry mouth, fewer tears when crying, darker urine or fewer wet diapers, and sunken eyes; in an infant, a sunken soft spot on top of the head is a warning sign. Seek care early if you see these.

## Why vaccination matters

Vaccines protect children against serious, preventable infections such as measles, polio, diphtheria, whooping cough, and meningitis. Sticking to the recommended schedule is one of the safest and most effective steps you can take for your child's health. Discuss any missed or skipped doses with your paediatrician.

## When to seek urgent care — RED FLAGS

**A child needs urgent paediatric attention if they:**
- Are very sleepy, hard to wake, lethargic, or unusually floppy.
- Have not passed urine in many hours (or have had no wet diaper for over 6–8 hours in an infant).
- Have a fever with a stiff neck, a rash that does not fade when pressed, or a seizure.
- Are breathing fast or with difficulty, or have blue-tinged lips.
- Show sunken eyes, no tears when crying, or are refusing to drink at all.
- In a baby, have a soft spot that is sunken, or any fever in a newborn (under 3 months) — seek advice promptly.

Trust your instincts: a fussy or poorly-appearing child who is not perking up deserves a quick check by a doctor.
